All work

Python · PostgreSQL · pgvector · RAG

Knowledge pipeline

Breaks scattered notes into small units with an LLM, links and clusters them with embeddings, and writes from them, flagging weakly grounded output. It writes the HW Insight blog

Context

A system that gathers scattered notes — chats, web clippings, voice memos — and turns them into writing grounded in its sources.

Problem

Give an LLM a pile of material and ask for an article, and plausible but unsupported sentences slip in. Large inputs sometimes weren't processed at all.

What I built

  • Collects from Telegram, Slack, the web, RSS, email and voice (speech-to-text), stores originals encrypted, and loads each original only once.
  • An LLM breaks material into the smallest meaningful units and enriches them. Each unit's embedding is stored in pgvector, and similarity is used to link related units and cluster them.
  • For each cluster, an insight is drawn and scored for grounding in the sources; low scores are flagged. Automatically written posts start from these insights.
  • There is also a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) path: a query is embedded, related units are retrieved from pgvector, and the article is written from them.
  • Work runs on a PostgreSQL queue, with backoff retries and recovery of stalled jobs.

What I got wrong

  • Notes over 40,000 characters were finishing with zero units extracted. After I split long notes into chunks, one such note (51 chunks) yielded 580 units.
  • Measuring along the way showed that the embedding server silently truncated long inputs without an error, so the client now enforces its own input-length cap.
  • I also measured batch size. Latency grew linearly with the number of items (150 items: 12.7 s; 1,200: 101 s), and 300 items already came close to the 30-second request timeout. To leave headroom under load, embeddings go out in sequential batches of 150.

Result

17 posts published on the HW Insight blog, in Korean and English (as of 2026-11-08).
