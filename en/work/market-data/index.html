Market data & paper-trading system

For my own investing. Collects and validates Korean market data every trading day and runs paper trading. After an exchange rule change, I spotted and corrected 25,384 contaminated closing prices that had passed every check

Context

A system I built for my own investing. It collects and validates Korean stock prices and investor-flow data every trading day, then runs backtests and paper trading on that data.

Problem

A collection job finishing as "done" didn't mean its output was right. A stalled feed could return zero rows and still be logged as complete, and holiday rows that shouldn't exist could appear.

What I built

  • Twenty-four weekday batch jobs handle collection, correction, feature computation, backtests and paper orders.
  • About 8.86 million price rows (4,001 stocks) in PostgreSQL. Loads are idempotent, and interrupted backfills resume from where they stopped.
  • Backtests run on point-in-time universes that include delisted stocks, so results aren't inflated by survivorship bias.
  • Feed status is recorded as fresh, stalled or empty, and an update is refused if the stock count suddenly drops.
  • 3,033 test functions; changes ship through CI, a schema check, then deploy.

Figures as of 2026-10-27; the row count is the figure recorded in the repository's documentation.

What I got wrong

  • To get fixes in faster, I widened the range of bug fixes that deploy automatically (Sep 2026).
  • The next morning an automatic deploy restarted the system mid-collection, and a day of price data went missing.
  • I kept automatic deploys but restricted when they can happen: no deploys during the early-morning collection window, and a test checks every time that the window still covers the real collection schedule.

Result

  • After the Korea Exchange opened an after-hours session in September 2026, 75% of the closing prices I collected no longer matched the official close. Every check and alert still passed; the investigation started because I suspected the change in trading hours. I moved collection to right after the market close and corrected 25,384 contaminated rows. After the correction, all 144 sampled prices (12 stocks × 12 days) matched the brokerage's official closing prices.
  • Backed up and removed 1,000 investor-flow rows that had appeared on market holidays.
